## 📝 Descrição

#### Current Behavior:
---
- Call the PATCH recurring-consents endpoint
- Expect 200 - Validate Response
- Call the GET recurring-payments sending the recurringConsentID on the header

In our solution we need some time to change the status of the payments and confirm the cancellation in the core bank's backend. In this test, PATCH was called at 3:25:17 PM and GET at 3:25:19 PM, so depending on the stress of the backend we didn't pass the test.

#### Expected Behavior:
---
Create a delay of 30 seconds or some polling on the GET recurring-payments

Hi,

According to the documentation (see screenshot), when a long-duration consent is revoked via the PATCH /recurring-consents endpoint, if the operation is successful, the consent must immediately transition to the status "REVOKED".

"Caso bem sucedido, o consentimento vai para o status 'REVOKED'"

![image](/uploads/30e08de24ade2a45764b98ea162666b3/image.png)

This implies that the status change must be synchronous and immediate, at least from the API's perspective.

However, in our implementation, the update in the backend (core banking system) takes a few seconds to propagate. In the specific test case, the PATCH was executed at 15:25:17, and the GET /recurring-payments was executed just 2 seconds later at 15:25:19, before the status had been fully updated.

The Open Finance Brasil documentation does not explicitly allow for polling or delayed confirmation in this context. The expectation is that, once the PATCH /recurring-consents call is successful, the consent must immediately reflect the "REVOKED" status and any associated recurring payments must reflect the updated state accordingly.
