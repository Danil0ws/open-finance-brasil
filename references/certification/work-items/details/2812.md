## 📝 Descrição

**Context**

The transactions validator for Accounts v2.5 requires the **codeISPB** field whenever **partieCnpjCpf** is present, regardless of the transaction type. In the specification, codeISPB is defined as the ISPB of the counterparty institution of the transaction, to be filled according to creditDebitType: for CREDITO, the institution of origin of the funds, for DEBITO, the institution of destination of the funds. The restriction states it is mandatory when counterparty information exists.

**Our interpretation**

We read codeISPB as the ISPB of the institution through which the counterparty receives or sends the funds (the origin or destination institution), and not as the ISPB of the person or entity identified in partieCnpjCpf. These are distinct: partieCnpjCpf identifies the counterparty person (payer or payee), while codeISPB identifies the institution the funds passed through.

**Observed behavior**

The validator raises "Field codeISPB is required when field partieCnpjCpf is present" for any transaction that has partieCnpjCpf populated and codeISPB absent, regardless of transaction type and direction.

**Questions**

1. Is the interpretation correct that codeISPB is the ISPB of the institution of origin or destination of the funds (the counterparty's institution), and not the ISPB of the person or entity identified in partieCnpjCpf?
2. Should the codeISPB requirement be conditioned on the transaction type and direction, aligned with the counterparty table, rather than solely on the presence of partieCnpjCpf?

We received clarification from the OPF following consultation with the BCB.

The `codeISPB` identifies the institution used by the payer/payee and should therefore always be provided, including for the PJ scenario reported in this issue.

Based on this clarification, the absence of `codeISPB` in the reported case is not expected.
