## 📝 Descrição

Current behavior:

The test validates if the `cancelation.canceledBy.document.identification` is equal to `data.creditors.cpfCnpj`, even if it's a revogation with reason `REVOGADO_USUARIO`.

Expected behavior:

From the API documentations, it's expected to be the document from who is paying, as it says in the details field from the revocation request `REVOGADO_USUARIO: O usuário pagador revogou a recorrência do consentimento;`

Hi @NanerX. Thank you for the follow-up.

In this specific scenario (**revokedBy: USUARIO, revokedFrom: INICIADORA**), the cancellation is initiated by a natural person, the payer, and the available identifier to determine who actually performed the revocation is the loggedUser.document.identification from the original consent.

Even when the consent is associated with a PESSOA_JURIDICA via the businessEntity, the actual approval and revocation are both carried out by a specific individual (CPF), as defined in the loggedUser field, as we can see in the example from @ranieri85.

Therefore, for this test, we consider loggedUser.document.identification as the correct reference for the comparison with cancelledBy.document.identification. Using the businessEntity document in this very specific test case would not accurately reflect who performed the action.

If the intention is to support a scenario where a consent could be revoked by the business entity without a linked CPF, even under revokedBy: USUARIO, that could be brought to the Working Group (WG) for discussion.

We also want to let you know that **the fix for the current test logic has been deployed**. Feel free to rerun it and confirm the resolution.

Best regards

### Comentário 15
**Autor**: Daniela Muller (@danielamllr)
**Criado em**: 09/10/2025, 16:13:31

Hi,

The conformance suit's expectation is based on the rule that the field `cancellation.cancelledBy.document.identification` of the payment **must be populated with the creditor's document** (the CNPJ from `data.creditors` in the consent), even if the revocation was requested by the user-payer (`REVOGADO_USUARIO`).

Your server's response in GET **recurring-consents**:

![image.png](/uploads/9c09e4009d9497098c4e7e73d80994b1/image.png)

This rule follows the structural definition of the `cancelledBy` and `document` fields:

* **`cancelledBy`**: The `document` field within `cancelledBy` refers to the **payer or receiver** who requested the payment cancellation.

![image.png](/uploads/22a245b77e799a6668d9c0281e02eff3/image.png)

* **Context of Payer Revocation (`REVOGADO_USUARIO`):** When the user-payer revokes the consent, all future scheduled payments are canceled. The test expects the **creditor's document** to be used to link the cancellation to the primary entity of the original consent.

To execute successfully the test, you must adjust your implementation so that in the response to the `GET /recurring-payments/{recurringPaymentId}` after the revocation:

The field **`cancellation.cancelledBy.document.identification`** of the canceled payment (status **`CANC`**) is populated with the **creditor's `cpfCnpj`** (the same CNPJ present in `data.creditors` of the consent).

Feel free to reach out in case of further questions!

**Best regards,**
